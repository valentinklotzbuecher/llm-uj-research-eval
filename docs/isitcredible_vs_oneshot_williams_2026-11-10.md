# isitcredible review vs one-shot models on Williams et al. (2024)

*AI-run analysis (Claude Code, Opus 5.5), 10 November 2026. One paper, AI judges only, exploratory. Evaluators are referred to as E1 and E2. Every number below is copied from `results/isitcredible_vs_oneshot_williams_2026-11-10/summary.md`, generated by `scripts/compare_isitcredible_williams.py summarize`; rerun that to check them.*

## Bottom line

On this paper, Joe Francis's isitcredible `review` run did **not** find the Unjournal evaluators' issues better than one-shot models. It found almost none of them at the "same concern" level, while one-shot Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.2 Pro found a third to two-thirds, depending on the judge. What isitcredible did well was different: sharper statistical mechanisms aimed at the headline numbers (calibration of the area total, spatially autocorrelated validation, the confidence interval, the threshold rule). Those target the same results the evaluators questioned, but for different reasons. Most of the one-shot models' list made the same moves in less depth.

## Leakage: the run saw the Nature referee reports

- Joe confirmed the run had the Nature peer review file (referee reports and rebuttals). So on any issue a Nature referee raised, the comparison measures recall, not discovery.
- Whether it saw the Unjournal evaluations is not confirmed. Its synthesis omits the evaluators' main concerns (predictor timing and the soil predictor's link to land cover), which is weak evidence it did not use them.
- Only the final `SYNTHESIS.md` was shared: no stage reports, scripts, logs, module list or suite version. Cost and run time were not recorded (the suite usually takes about a day). The run has not been audited (`audit-review` was not run on it).
- Control used here: the Nature referee issues (37, from R1, R2 and R3) were extracted and judged against the evaluators' issues. "Not copyable" means the evaluator issues that no Nature referee raised under either judge at a given level: 20 of 25 at strict, 17 at standard, 3 at loose.
- The one-shot models never saw the referee file, yet 27–73% of their items (strict and standard levels, depending on judge) also repeat a Nature referee point. So the overlap with the referees partly reflects what any careful reader of this paper raises, not only copying.

## What each caught that the other missed

- **Evaluator issues isitcredible covered that the Opus one-shot did not:** none, under either judge at any level.
- **Covered by a one-shot model and not by isitcredible** (standard; both replicates for one list, neither for the other): 10 issues (Claude judge) / 13 (GPT judge). Taken across both judges, they are the evaluators' central points: predictors measured during the outcome period and the soil predictor built from land cover (leakage, E1/E2), socioeconomic confounding and the "lower bound" reading of 215 Mha (E1), regrowth persistence and concurrent deforestation (E1), additionality of the targeting advice (E1), stability of post-2000 settlement and roads (E2), the inflated accuracy that follows from leakage (E2), alternative algorithms (E2), and the limits of a biophysical-only map for prioritization (E2).
- **What isitcredible's matches were:** under the Claude judge, its pairs with evaluator issues were almost all "same target, different problem": it questioned the same numbers (accuracy, 215 Mha, the CI, the training labels) through different mechanisms. Under the GPT judge its only stable standard-level matches were additionality (via its carbon-ceiling item, which a Nature referee also raised) and omission in the input regrowth map (via its incomplete-labels item, which two Nature referees raised in other words).
- **What isitcredible added that neither evaluator raised:** calibration of class-balanced random-forest scores summed as expected area; low producer's accuracy for the regeneration class; the 98%-within-300 m reading of the map as largely a forest-proximity buffer; and two deposit computations (a threshold sweep, and a failure to reproduce the reported areas from the deposited rasters). The calibration point matches what `persistent_misses.qmd` already reports from the one-shot model, so it is not unique to isitcredible.

## Evidence behind isitcredible-only findings (step 5)

Hand-coded in `isitcredible_evidence_check.json`. No script or log behind any finding was shared, so no computational claim could be rerun.

- Calibration, spatial autocorrelation and the threshold rule start from numbers the paper or SI prints; the direction of each argument checks out against the text, the size does not without a refit. These agree with `persistent_misses.qmd` (calibration; 75–82% accuracy away from training points), so nothing here contradicts it.
- The threshold-sweep finding compares the range of totals across thresholds with the paper's CI. A sweep measures sensitivity to the definition of "area", not sampling uncertainty, so the "far wider than the CI" framing mixes two quantities (as `docs/reviewer2_extension.qmd` already notes). The sensitivity itself is worth reporting.
- The non-reproduction finding may be a projection or domain artifact: the synthesis itself says the deposit is in lat/lon and unclipped. It is a reproducibility gap, not a demonstrated error in the paper.
- The producer's-accuracy range is not printed in the paper; its derivation was not shared.
- Exact isitcredible numbers and quotes are kept out of the public repo (`private/`, ignored): Joe has not released them and said he would check them before they were publicized.

## Module recommendation

- **ML/prediction module: worth writing.** The general stages produced good statistical critique of the reported numbers, but missed the problems specific to a predictive model: predictors measured during or derived from the outcome (temporal and target leakage), what the validation design can and cannot show once leakage is present, and extrapolating to 2030 under stable covariates. These were the evaluators' main concerns and the one-shot models raised several of them. A checklist stage on feature timing, feature provenance and the validation design would likely have caught them.
- **Meta-analysis, forecasting/elicitation, cost-effectiveness modules:** this paper says nothing about them.
- **Caveats on that recommendation:** one paper; AI judges only; the isitcredible run had the referee file and is unaudited, of unknown version and configuration; and part of the gap may be the suite's focus on the headline numbers rather than on the policy reading, which the evaluators stressed. Before writing modules, rerunning on two or three more papers (without the referee file, with stage reports kept) would show whether the gap is general.
