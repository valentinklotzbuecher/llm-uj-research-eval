# Copyright Policy Options for Generative Artificial Intelligence

Evidence refreshed October 5, 2026. AI-assisted prioritization note; not an official evaluation.

**Joshua Gans. Recommendation: low-to-medium priority.**

The working paper dates to 2024, but the article was published in the Journal of Law and Economics in March 2026:
https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/733761

The policy area remains highly active; for example, the UK government published a major copyright-and-AI report in March 2026:
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence

The fresh search did not establish that the UK report relies on Gans's paper. Topic relevance therefore should not be converted into paper-specific uptake.

A law-and-economics theorist could examine transaction-cost assumptions, representation of licensing markets, creator incentives, training-quality effects, and how the stylized regimes map to current legal choices.

Recommendation: respectable theory with fresh journal publication, but lower priority than papers with clearer current downstream use.
