# Aggregate prioritization update — October 5, 2026

This is a working prioritization update, not an official Unjournal decision.

## Main ranking change

SaferAI's paper on evaluating frontier safety frameworks should move from alternate status into roughly the first dozen candidates.

Reason: the International AI Safety Report 2026 cites the paper directly, giving it stronger verified policy-synthesis uptake than the earlier dashboard note reflected. This does not imply endorsement of the paper's scores by participating governments.

The paper is also unusually suitable for independent evaluation because its rubric, criteria, weights, and provider-level scores can be audited and stress-tested.

## Other changes

Who Evaluates AI's Social Impacts? remains a strong methods reserve. Its latest version was accepted at ICML 2026 and has a larger third-party sample than earlier versions. Direct government use was not verified.

AI in a Fragmenting World should rank somewhat higher than its media profile suggests because the NBER page records support from UK and South Korean government research programmes. This is funding, not adoption.

Several other papers are closely related to live policy debates but still lack verified paper-specific uptake. Topic relevance should remain separate from evidence that decision-makers are using a particular study.

## Current first group

The first group remains led by:
1. Forecasting the Economic Effects of AI.
2. Epoch's compute-governance measurement study.
3. The AGI Race and Existential Risk.
4. The Economics of Recursive Self-Improvement.
5. Delays to Frontier AI in the EU and UK.
6. METR's task-horizon work, with an explicit recency exception.

The exact ordering is a planning judgment, not a quality score.

## Evidence standard

Funding is not endorsement. Conference or institutional hosting is not adoption. A citation is not validation. Media coverage is not policy use. General attention to a topic is not paper-specific uptake.
