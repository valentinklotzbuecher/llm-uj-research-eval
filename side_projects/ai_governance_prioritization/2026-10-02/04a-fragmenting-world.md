# AI in a Fragmenting World

[Back to overview](README.md)

**Evidence refreshed October 5, 2026. AI-assisted prioritization note, not an official Unjournal evaluation.**

**Barry Eichengreen and coauthors. Proposed queue: about 14. Strong geopolitics/economics reserve.**

The paper studies how geoeconomic fragmentation may alter international AI diffusion and gains, with particular attention to MENAP economies. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35597).

The strongest verified attention signal is funding proximity: NBER states that the work was partly supported by the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office and a South Korean Ministry of Economy and Finance research programme. This is government research funding, not evidence that either government adopted the conclusions. The paper is also a [CEPR Discussion Paper](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21867), which is meaningful economics-policy circulation but not policy use. Major paper-specific media attention was not verified.

An international economist should examine the modeling of fragmentation, trade and technology frictions, AI preparedness, the connector-economy result, and the robustness of regional conclusions. The key distinction is between a modeled possibility and an empirical prediction about a specific country.

**Bottom line:** above-average candidate, especially if the supporting research programmes or regional policymakers identify concrete decisions.
