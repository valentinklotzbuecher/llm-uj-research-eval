# How to Count AIs: Individuation and Liability for AI Agents

Evidence refreshed October 5, 2026. AI-assisted prioritization note; not an official evaluation.

**Yonathan Arbel, Peter Salib, and Simon Goldstein. Proposed queue: about 17.**

The paper argues that legal accountability for AI agents requires reliable ways to connect actions to accountable principals and to distinguish persistent agent-like entities. It proposes an "Algorithmic Corporation" as one possible legal structure. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10028

**Attention:** the Institute for Law & AI hosts the article on its research site: https://law-ai.org/how-to-count-ais-individuation-and-liability-for-ai-agents/ . The work is also listed as a Boston College Law Review article and has entered specialist corporate-governance discussion. These are legal/policy-institution circulation signals, not government adoption. Direct government use and major independent media coverage were not verified.

**What an evaluation could add:** a corporate-law or law-and-economics scholar should separate the diagnosis of identity problems from the positive case for the proposed entity solution and test whether ordinary entity, agency, authentication, and liability rules already solve much of the problem.

**Bottom line:** interesting and increasingly discussed legal theory, but a focused feasibility review is probably more efficient than a flagship evaluation.
